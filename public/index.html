How appliance running costs are worked out

Electricity is sold by the kilowatt-hour (kWh): using 1,000 watts for one hour. To find what an appliance costs, work out how many kWh it uses and multiply by your unit rate:

  • kWh = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours used
  • Cost = kWh × unit rate

A 2,500 W tumble dryer running for an hour uses 2.5 kWh. At 26.32p per kWh that’s about 66p a load, or around £34 a year at one load a week.

The Ofgem price cap from 1 October 2026

Ofgem sets a cap on the unit rates and standing charges suppliers can charge on standard variable (default) tariffs. From 1 October to 31 December 2026 the average capped electricity price for Direct Debit customers in England, Scotland and Wales is 26.32p per kWh with a standing charge of 54.83p a day. VAT has been removed from electricity bills from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027. From 1 July to 30 September 2026 the rates were 26.11p per kWh and 57.19p a day, including 5% VAT.

These are averages. Your rate depends on your region, how you pay and your meter. Fixed tariffs, Economy 7 and prepayment meters can be different, so type in the unit rate from your bill if you know it. Northern Ireland isn’t covered by the Ofgem cap.

Getting an accurate figure

Many appliances don’t run at full power all the time. Heaters, ovens and fridges switch on and off with their thermostat, and a washing machine uses most of its energy heating water at the start of a cycle. For these, the yearly kWh on the energy label (or a plug-in energy monitor) is more accurate than the maximum wattage.

Energy running cost FAQs

How do I work out how much an appliance costs to run?

Divide its power in watts by 1,000 to get kilowatts, multiply by the hours you use it to get kilowatt-hours (kWh), then multiply by your unit rate. A 2,000 W heater used for 3 hours uses 6 kWh, which costs £1.58 at 26.32p per kWh.

What is the electricity price cap unit rate now?

From 1 October to 31 December 2026 the Ofgem price cap averages 26.32p per kWh for electricity, with a daily standing charge of 54.83p, for people paying by Direct Debit in England, Scotland and Wales. There’s no VAT on electricity from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027.

How much does it cost to boil a kettle?

A 3 kW kettle that runs for 4 minutes uses 0.2 kWh. At 26.32p per kWh that’s about 5.3p. Boiling only the water you need cuts the cost.

Is the price cap a limit on my bill?

No. It caps the unit rate and standing charge on standard variable tariffs, not your total bill. The more you use, the more you pay, and your rate depends on your region, meter and how you pay.

Does this include the standing charge?

The appliance costs don’t, because you pay the standing charge every day whatever you use. The calculator shows it separately so you can see your full yearly electricity cost.

How much does a 2 kW heater cost per hour?

At 26.32p per kWh, a 2 kW (2,000 W) heater costs about 52.6p for every hour it runs at full power. Thermostats cycle on and off, so the real cost is often lower.

Where do I find my appliance’s wattage?

Look for the rating plate or sticker on the back or base, or check the manual. Fridges and washing machines also show an energy label with yearly kWh, which is more accurate than their maximum wattage.
